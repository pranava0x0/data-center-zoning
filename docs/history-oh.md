# Ohio: where to search and what the public records establish

**141 dated or undated records** were recovered from four municipal governments and Ohio EPA in the November 15, 2026 snapshot; 129 show a date and 12 do not. There are no invented January 1 dates. The unit is a public permit, case or state permit-register entry, not a built or operating data center. The [full records](../output/history/ohio/history.json), [coverage audit and held candidate numbers](../data/history/ohio/coverage.json), [regional rankings](../data/history/ohio/rank.json) and [collector](../scripts/collectors/ohio/history.py) are independently usable.

## Why the Columbus method transfers

The municipal GIS published **690,297 building/trade rows** but no searchable work narrative: `VALUE_DESC` and applicant-name searches returned zero direct facility-phrase candidates. The **6,555-row engineering layer** has a work narrative: an inclusive SQL screen returned 11 candidates; whole-phrase matching across individual fields kept 10, on two parcels. We collected **all 166 building/trade numbers** on those parcels across the available vintages, including permits earlier than the engineering file. We reconstructed the current [city Accela](https://portal.columbus.gov/Permits/Welcome.aspx) record URL using the three case identifiers in the original city GIS link. For each result, the printed record number must match the queried case. Only the **Project Description field**, not an applicant, address or navigation item, can establish a direct match. All 166 pages were archived, 69 descriptions qualified, and 97 candidates were held with their reason. A site match alone is not promoted. Example: [NEWC2203324](https://portal.columbus.gov/Permits/Cap/CapDetail.aspx?Module=Building&TabName=Building&capID1=22CAP&capID2=00000&capID3=001E1&agencyCode=COLUMBUS) explicitly describes a shell for a future data-center building and excludes specified MEP work.

New Albany's [monthly city project list](https://newalbanyohio.org/community-development/project-updates/) supplies an independent [queue of 38 named leads](../data/history/ohio/new_albany_leads.json) explicitly described as new data centers/buildings. It does not supply dated permit identifiers and is **not** included in the 141. Its numerical equality with the independent PNNL municipality count is coincidental; there is no established one-to-one crosswalk. New Albany's [permitting department](https://newalbanyohio.org/community-resources/permitting-inspections/) has separate building, zoning and engineering review. The public [boards dashboard](https://newalbanyohio.org/community-development/project-updates/) contains 585 entitlements across uses and is **not** an archive of every New Albany building permit. The [April 2026 STACK packet](https://newalbanyohio.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/26-0420-PC-Meeting-Packet.pdf) distinguishes a finalized plan from subsequent modifications: do not assume the GIS disposition date always identifies a particular meeting vote.

## Scope and gaps

Ohio EPA facility names were screened across individual (3,146 rows), general (8,497), industrial stormwater (3,594), active construction (8,725), inactive construction (51,909), indirect discharges (148) and no-exposure (5,440) layers, without an initial date restriction. Only active/inactive construction returned exact matches in this snapshot. One SQL false positive referred to a data **science** building; it was held. Codenames and generic owners cause misses. The 52 state register entries are not the state's unique facilities or the entire state permitting history. County assignments are from the agency facility-county field; the reported city is kept separately and is not assumed to establish municipal jurisdiction. Codes for draft, revoked or expired coverage are retained; a null date is unknown. Date-only Ohio EPA values are serialized by UTC calendar date, without treating midnight as the previous local day; municipal layers declare Eastern time with DST. The [air permit search](https://epa.ohio.gov/divisions-and-offices/air-pollution-control/permitting/issued-air-permits) includes multiple stages of a permit, and the one final air document here is a sample, not a statewide air-docket extraction.

**Priorities for the next pass:** (1) reconcile the 38 municipal New Albany leads to numbered Accela cases and record possible duplicate phases; (2) retrieve Jerome Township's township-issued certificates and then independently match **Union County** building permits; (3) enumerate all Hilliard/Dublin planning minutes and expand Columbus parcels through archived rezoning decisions; (4) seed the statewide environmental search with verified operator aliases. Ohio's third-ranked county illustrates why a postal-city assignment fails: [Jerome Township](https://www.jerometownship.com/departments/zoning) explicitly distinguishes its zoning certificates from permits of the county building department. [Township certificate 10826](https://iworq-upload.s3.amazonaws.com/JEROMETOWNSHIP/601/30015166-ZC%20%2310826%20-%20Approved%20Cert.%2C%20App.%2C%20%26%20Plans%20-%20Part%201.pdf) identifies a data-center complex and a December 22, 2025 approval at a Plain City **mailing** address. The public portal and PDF hosting service each returned HTTP 403 on the robots preflight to this collector, so the record is documented as a priority lead but is **not** counted as an archived permit. No countywide completeness, operation or capacity is inferred from these events.

## Reproduce

From the project folder with the project Python dependencies installed:

```sh
python3 scripts/collectors/ohio/history.py                          # deterministic offline build
python3 scripts/collectors/ohio/history.py --fetch                  # refresh all sources including record pages
python3 scripts/collectors/ohio/history.py --fetch --reuse-portal   # deliberately preserve old Accela captures for matching IDs
```

The [source config](../data/history/ohio/config.json) fixes endpoints, field selection, queries and cutoff; the [GIS/PDF manifest](../data/history/ohio/manifest.json), [individual record manifest](../data/history/ohio/portal_manifest.json) and [reviewed-document registry](../data/history/ohio/documents.json) retain locators and SHA-256. The ID-first ArcGIS procedure from Virginia carries over: enumerate all candidate IDs, check schema, batch the smallest necessary public attributes, verify exact ID-set equality and down-select using deterministic whole-phrase rules. The Columbus portal adapter and the New Albany status-domain translation are jurisdiction-specific additions. Full public portal pages are stored gzip-compressed locally; published rows contain selected permit information and evidence excerpts only. All six documentary PDFs have independently checkable citations: five have matching source text and the Dublin building-4 decision is scanned, visually verified on [page 1](../cache/history/oh/dublin4-reviewed-page1.png) and pinned to exact bytes. A changed scanned PDF fails closed for fresh review. Preserve the manifests alongside the cache before refreshing if retaining multiple dated releases.

For the unincorporated area of Jersey Township, [the resolution](https://www.jerseytownship.us/zoning-resolution-book.html) requires township zoning approval before the county building permit; the [state statute](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-519.02) limits township zoning to unincorporated territory. The [application form](https://www.jerseytownship.us/zoning-permit-application.html) and §2.05 give different expiration terms. No single expiration period or township approval for annexed New Albany sites was inferred.
