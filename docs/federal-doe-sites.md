# DOE site decisions: seven NEPA determinations and three land/developer milestones

These 10 records describe five civilian data-center sites through November 15, 2026. They contribute **ten federal history events and zero regulatory permits**. A National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) categorical exclusion establishes DOE’s environmental determination for the specified action. A proposed land transfer, completed real-property lease, developer selection and building permit are four different things. Neither announced capacity nor construction completion is inferred.

**Scope of search and exclusions.** The [DOE Portsmouth/Paducah office index](https://www.energy.gov/nepa/listings/categorical-exclusion-determinations-portsmouth-paducah-project-office) was searched against the two identified parcels and named projects. Paducah Parcel A’s enrichment facility, other Parcel C industrial redevelopment, generic environmental monitoring, general solicitations, groundbreaking ceremonies, and nearby public works were not coded as approvals for the data-center campus. The historical research-computing cases are targeted examples, not an exhaustive DOE inventory. A later GAO [land inventory](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-27-108063.pdf) lists the 785-acre Kentucky lease as 2026, but gives no execution day. It is supplemental, not the archived source of a dated event. No conclusions are drawn about absent permits, preemption of state law, current operation, commissioning or the status of projects on other parcels.

**Reproduction.** Run `python3 scripts/collectors/federal/doe_nepa.py` from the project root. It replays the signed forms, dated agency pages and location references from `cache/history/doe_more/` against the URL/byte-count/SHA-256 manifest `data/history/federal/doe_nepa/sources.json`. `--fetch` adds missing originals under the project’s regular robots policy. PDFs are read with PyMuPDF to include the signed forms’ filled fields; relying only on a PDF’s static boilerplate loses the location, action and dates. Source changes that remove an expected case number, date or scope anchor fail closed. Location corroboration provides geographic evidence only and generates no extra history events.

Outputs are `output/history/federal/doe_nepa/california.json` (1), `history/federal/doe_nepa/idaho.json` (1), `history/federal/doe_nepa/illinois.json` (1), `history/federal/doe_nepa/kentucky.json` (4), and `history/federal/doe_nepa/ohio.json` (3). Every row links an original, pins its bytes and gives a scope-specific locator; all PDF decisions also link their DOE case pages. NEPA rows reuse the existing register/classification. The three developer/lease milestones are handled by `federal_doe_more.classify`, separately from the Interior Department’s right-of-way register. The assembler tests every row for non-permit treatment, checks the seven case numbers against the pre-existing DOE cases, and verifies there are 10 distinct IDs. The two lease records use explicit descriptive labels instead of inventing an agency instrument number. Neither lease is counted as a construction or operating permit.
