# State and territorial construction stormwater screening

The November 16, 2026 capture covers all 50 states, DC, and five inhabited territories in the atlas roster. Forty state provisions with verified acreage screens and three narrower provisions passed a byte/text/evidence check. Five states already had authoring records (VA, TX, AZ, OH, IA; shared validation decides whether each is displayed), eight use EPA's primary construction general permit; the three narrower records do not establish an acreage trigger. This is a screen for planning construction, not a count of permits, proof of coverage at a site, legal advice about a parcel, or evidence that a use is permitted by zoning.

Start at [EPA’s state authorization directory](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/authorization-status-epas-construction-and-industrial-stormwater-programs) and [EPA’s agency directory](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/contact-us-stormwater), then use the **modified federal permit itself**—[2022 CGP, Appendix B](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-04/appendix-b-areas-of-permit-coverage.pdf)—to identify EPA-retained areas. The eight primary federal jurisdictions are AS, DC, GU, MA, MP, NH, NM and PR; each program number is confirmed against the appendix. Federal areas may exist within other states as well: Indian country and certain federal lands/operators have location- and program-specific rules. The older authorization table still describes Idaho as EPA-administered; Appendix B and [Idaho DEQ’s delegated IPDES program](https://www.deq.idaho.gov/permits/water-quality-permits-certifications/ipdes-permits/) distinguish tribal lands from ordinary sites. Contacts are links to office listings, not attestations to who currently holds an agency job.

The rows use each state regulator's own program description, permit, regulation or statute and include a short matched passage, locator, numeric screening thresholds, and a common-plan review. “Not established in passage” does **not** mean exempt. Single-project and aggregate acreage, separately filed notice requirements, site conditions, and the timing of land disturbance are different. For example, [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/egle/about/organization/water-resources/soil-erosion/construction-stormwater-overview) describes permit-by-rule coverage and the separate notice at five acres, [New York](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/water/water-quality/stormwater/construction-activity-permit) distinguishes disturbed soil from newly impervious area, [Connecticut](https://portal.ct.gov/deep/water-regulating-and-discharges/stormwater/construction-stormwater-gp) distinguishes municipal review from state registration, and [Maine’s statute](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/38/title38sec420-D.html) requires separate project approval in organized areas. Do not apply the numeric base threshold alone to a small lot in a larger campus. Operating-phase industrial discharges and construction-phase runoff are separate programs.

Colorado’s general point-source-discharge statute (2026 C.R.S. §25-8-501), Kansas’s 45-day decision deadline (K.S.A. 75-5676) and the Virgin Islands’ NOI/engineer-stamped SWPPP instructions are published separately as narrower provisions. None is counted as a verified CGP acreage threshold. Their coverage dispositions are explicit in the [jurisdiction inventory](../data/state_construction_coverage.json). Challenges include an unsuccessful public robots preflight, no trigger in a captured page, and inconsistent program-page and dated permit wording. A held state is not unregulated. State-specific office anchors remain available through the EPA directory. Known time-sensitive example: Nebraska's archived general permit gives November 30, 2026 as its expiration. Recheck the successor before relying on that document for later construction.

## Replication and review

Run from the project root:

    python3 scripts/ordinances/rules/state_construction_rules.py             # offline; verify and derive JSON
    python3 scripts/ordinances/rules/state_construction_rules.py --snippets --state NY
    python3 scripts/ordinances/rules/state_construction_rules.py --refresh --state NY  # public source; honors robots
    python3 scripts/ordinances/rules/state_construction_rules.py --stage-cache         # copy only verified hash-named objects

[Candidate URLs](../data/state_construction_candidates.json) are curated by state, while the [review sheet](../data/state_construction_review.tsv) contains source choices and distinct, human-reviewed conclusions. Fetches archive raw bytes, extracted text, links, URL, adapter, timestamp, SHA-256 and extraction SHA-256 in [the isolated archive](../cache/state-construction/). Do not promote query snippets, a failed fetch, or a search lead. For a source revision, review the neighboring sections as well as the anchored phrase; only then update shared pins. The deterministic build checks both byte hashes, every quoted anchor, PDF page numbers where applicable, every EPA geographic identifier and that every jurisdiction has exactly one disposition.

For atlas integration, include `state_construction_rules` in the rule-file enumeration. After `--stage-cache`, merge only the entries of `cache/state-construction/reviewed_index.json` into `output/source_index.json`: add absent keys; for URLs already in that index, reject a different byte **or** text hash for human reconciliation. Run the normal new-claim sealing and validation. Staging itself never edits that shared index or the evidence pins.
