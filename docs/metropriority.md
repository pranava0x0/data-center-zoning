# Central Ohio: gaps in municipal land-use histories

This pass adds 10 actions on seven numbered city cases: five actions in Hilliard (Franklin County) and five in New Albany (Licking County for these particular sites). They include one numbered temporary conditional-use permit family, zoning/variance decisions and a rezoning recommendation. The records are **not ten new permits, ten data-center sites or proof that construction commenced**.

Selection used the atlas's priority_localities.csv and [Ohio geographic crosswalk](history-oh.md). The observed-location layer has 32 markers in Franklin County and 31 in Licking County. New Albany spans both: 38 city markers, including seven in Franklin and 31 in Licking. Prior coverage showed six New Albany records by locality, including only four campus cases in its first municipal entitlement extraction. Hilliard has 15 markers, all in Franklin. Selecting both exposes local action which county-only or federal searches miss. The earlier [Dallas work](history-tx.md) already covered the modern city building register (70 issued records); a separate historical Dallas GIS register was not captured because its robots preflight returned HTTP 403. No Dallas records or new Dallas coverage are claimed here.

**Replay and controls.** Run **python3 scripts/collectors/ohio/central_ohio_entitlements.py**; add **--refresh** for fresh government captures. The adapter checks all 585 feature IDs against the city's own ID enumeration, reads published coded outcome labels, verifies case numbers against earlier releases, and checks page-bounded text anchors for the link between the facility, the proposed work and the actual disposition. Municipal status and individual board field are both retained. Text extraction alone did not support the Smart Farms identification: the analyst separately read exhibit A on page 27 and pinned the visually checked copy to its exact PDF hash. City-confirmed location is stored per record: New Albany is not assigned wholesale to either county. Hilliard's [charter, §1.01](https://hilliardohio.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Charter-2020-1.pdf) identifies Franklin County. Outputs do not include lots of neighboring facilities as evidence for the subject permit.

The full-register ID count does not mean every campus was identified. This is a curated second pass: an Amazon **fulfillment** sign variance (object 130651), restaurant entry at the same intersection (130866), and a withdrawn concrete plant at 2520 Beech (130427) without verified campus linkage were excluded. The existing withdrawn Verizon record (130597) has only a year and no adoption date; no January 1 was invented, and no duplicate was added. Tests cover competing votes in one docket, the later council vs. earlier planning date, reuse of a permit family, unrelated corporate names, earlier-history overlap and missing dates. A future refresh that changes a reviewed page, case identity or anchor needs review.

Source inventory and per-case evidence configuration: **data/national/metropriority_sources.json** and **metropriority_cases.json**. Response originals, extracted PDF texts, capture dates, lengths and SHA-256s: **cache/history/metropriority/manifest.json**. The combined source byte count is approximately 114 MB; a few multipart scanned packets dominate the cost. The ten normalized records are far smaller.
