# Reusing COMPASS citations as leads for a different technology

The five official workbooks are **evidence that the lab cited documents while examining energy technologies**. They are not observations about data centers. The transfer here is the *jurisdiction and document trail*. A rule is a separate record, written only after reading the actual data-center clause in the local ordinance.

## What was recovered

[The full crosswalk](../data/national/compass_code_sources.csv) has 5,806 rows (4.65 MB) spanning 34,466 original feature observations and 5,327 distinct normalized nonblank source URLs. Each output row retains the original state/county/subdivision/type and FIPS value as it was stored, original URL, original ordinance-year values and feature labels, Excel sheet and 1-based row ranges. FIPS are strings: import as text to preserve leading zeros. A row groups identical citation and geography **within one technology**. Input years in the original table are not claims of continued legal force or dates when data-center rules took effect.

## Geography and source authority are separate checks

The misleadingly broad column “County Subdivision FIPS Code” actually carries state placeholders, counties and independent cities, places, and county subdivisions. Validate the state/name against the [2025 Census Gazetteer](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/time-series/geo/gazetteer-files.html) per-row source, repair numeric leading zeros by observed width, and compare the parent county for 10-digit codes where supplied. An independent city with county-equivalent FIPS is preserved at the county-equivalent level. Seven-digit place IDs do not encode a county; the workflow does not pretend a supplied postal county was verified from that ID. No fuzzy remapping, centroids, address geocoding or same-name county substitution is used.

Of 5,806 crosswalk groups, **4,907** passed the state/geography/name tests against active place/subdivision, county or state layers (**3,683 distinct IDs**); **863** matched geographic units classed as statistical/non-governmental and are excluded from the local government queue; **36** remain unresolved. These are source-group counts, not counts of local zoning authorities. Even a valid Census civil division may lack local zoning power. The data can retain a code in its original field while declining to assign a present Census entity. See [full reason counts](../data/national/compass_code_sources_audit.json).

The gross URL roster includes separately labeled attribution classes: 88 groups have a matching government referral from the existing jurisdiction codebook directory, 1,034 match a jurisdiction website host, 1,501 have a government-looking host without verified issuing scope, 1,051 point to a code publisher without independently checked delegation, 2,082 are external or unattributed, and 50 have no usable URL. A .gov suffix can identify a government **host** without proving that government authored a local law: it can be a state planning commission hosting proposed text. A publisher can be the designated official publication without matching .gov; the conservative queue holds such references pending delegation. URL-level classifications are not judicial findings.

## Priority queue, bounded search and results

[The separate queue](../data/national/compass_code_sources_queue.csv) lists 1,013 distinct codebook-like PDF paths for non-statistical matched local geographies. Of these, 507 have either the jurisdiction's own matched host or a government referral; 506 are other government-host documents whose relationship to that locality still requires verification. Heuristics favor paths resembling general zoning books and suppress single-technology paths. Numbered unnamed PDFs and HTML codebooks may therefore be missed. A market directory sets state order and increases priority of mapped hotspots; its market counts are not government facts. Missouri was excluded from fetching under the project scope, but upstream Missouri provenance remains in the national crosswalk.

The first frozen cohort picked previously unchecked **URLs** in rotation through the ranked states (45 states), prioritizing markets and source attribution within each state, with a maximum of two files per host and geography. [The receipts](../data/national/compass_code_sources_screen.json) show 100 attempted: **4 keyword hits, 58 readable files with no matching text, 13 scans without machine-readable pages, 25 unreadable or blocked**. Duplicate-content flags identify when a different pre-existing project URL had identical PDF bytes; distinct URLs must not be counted as independent documents. The first pass archived about 161 MiB. A no-hit from a PDF does not prove absence of local requirements. A hit in a definition, agenda or draft does not establish a permit pathway.

Three referrals supported reviewed provisions:
- [Westmont, Illinois](https://westmont.illinois.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5043/VillageofWestmentZoningOrdinance-AsAmended-Feb-19-2026), page 72: read the table headers and row horizontally. Special use is marked for O/R, M-1 and M, not O/R-1; §6.05(B) separately defines the use. The [village's edition index](https://westmont.illinois.gov/580/2026-Amended-Zoning-Ordinance) identifies the code. The lead came from the heat-pump workbook.
- [Palmyra Township, Pike County, Pennsylvania](https://palmyrapike.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Palmyra-Twp-Zoning-Ord-2025-07-15.pdf), PDF p. 60: in HC, data centers appear under **conditional uses**, not the preceding list of principal permitted uses. The [township’s ordinance page](https://palmyrapike.org/ordinances/) links the July 15, 2025 book. The lead came from the wind workbook.
- [Mohave County, Arizona](https://www.mohave.gov/media/ll1dxipp/mohave-county-zoning-ordinance.pdf): §§14.D.8, 32.D.3, 33.D.3, 34.D.5 explicitly place data centers under special-use permitting in A-D, M-1, M-2 and M-X. Some entries continue onto the next page; checking only the matched paragraph would lose its controlling heading. Solar and wind cited the same government file.

A fourth referral, [Bulloch County’s 2023 zoning ordinance](https://bullochcounty.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Appendix-C-Zoning-ADOPTED-4_4_23-002-002.pdf), expressly mentions server farms/data centers in §1415(c) but the section is titled *Commercial Cryptocurrency Mining Operation*. A [later county-published amendment](https://bullochcounty.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Bulloch-County-Draft-Data-Center-Text-and-Map-Amendment-4.2026-w-overview-.pdf) is labeled **draft**. Neither is promoted here as evidence of an unrestricted current pathway or a new adopted moratorium. Follow up with the signed final action or extension. This is an example of why 4 keyword hits produced only 3 published-ready source findings.

## Replaying

From the project folder:

1. `python3 scripts/pipeline/compass_audit.py --fetch` acquires missing OEDI artifacts and rejects changed content; omit `--fetch` to validate existing files. Jurisdiction and market inputs are the project’s national Census and hotspot exports, not bundled into the energy workbook.
2. `python3 scripts/ordinances/compass/code_sources.py --screen --claims` rebuilds the crosswalk, queue, report and three hand-reviewed rule rows **offline**. `--reuse-export` skips reading the workbooks if only screening/rechecking. Published-ready data: [rules](../data/national/compass_code_sources_rules.json), [casebook with page and hash](../data/national/compass_code_sources_claims.json), and `cache/compass_code_sources/reviewed_index.json` for the master content-addressed index. The script never changes the shared index, Makefile or viewer.
3. On a new checkout, use `--fetch --limit 100 --workers 9` to execute the initial bounded PDF scan. The existing `cache/compass_code_sources/initial_cohort.json` fixes the cohort for subsequent runs; already checked records are not silently retried. The initial shared codebook check inventory may differ on another machine, so preserve that cohort for literal reproduction. The shared scanner honors robots and crawl delays and stops at 18 MiB per PDF. Content-address receipts make changes discoverable.
4. `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_compass_code_sources.py'` checks integral workbook coverage without double-counted Excel rows, original hashes, independent cities, missing zeros, and ambiguous same-name township parents.

## Checking the rest of the government codebooks

After the 100-file pilot, the [full referral pass](../data/national/compass_followup_status.json) examined all 1,013 eligible directly hosted PDFs in that queue, including the pilot. Of these, 635 had readable pages without a match, 58 mentioned a search term, 77 lacked readable text, and 243 were unavailable. See [the 58 matching files and page numbers](../data/national/compass_followup_matches.json). A match can be a proposed rule, an unrelated office, or an obsolete edition.

In Virginia, the county's own ordinance index led back to newer editions for [Pulaski](https://www.pulaskicounty.org/planning-zoning.html) and [Greene](https://www.greenecountyva.gov/184/Ordinances), plus [Franklin's Regional Enterprise Park chapter](https://franklincountyva.gov/342/Zoning-Ordinance). [The version ledger](../data/national/current_book_versions.json) retains the original NLR URLs alongside the local government's index and replacement bytes. Pulaski's permitted-use findings check the permitted marks against their column coordinates; Greene's definition alone is not a district approval.

[McAllen, Monroe and Habersham County](../data/national/second_codebook_rules.json) supplied eight provisions on conditional use, notice, minimum lot size, parking, noise and setbacks. [The alternate terminology check](alternate-published-rules.md) read 1,970 already archived documents, including local sources outside the COMPASS referral set. It found 49 matching documents and published 15 provisions from six jurisdictions. The [dispositions](../data/national/alternate_published_review.json) show which led only to ordinary offices, draft changes, or other unresolved evidence. For image-only codes, see the separate [OCR check](codebook-ocr.md).

For mapped provisions with explicit quantities, [the additional measures](../output/codebook_typed_measures.json) retain the comparator, unit, reference feature, exceptions, source page and exact check text. For example, Monroe's 500-foot building setback, 100-foot perimeter buffer and 500-foot notice radius are three different requirements. The atlas's [zoning pathways](../data/pathways.csv) also distinguish by-right use, special use, and conditional-district rezoning; a definition alone does not get any of those labels.
