# Arizona: 408 records, with approvals, current statuses and enforcement kept separate

The November 15, 2026 snapshot includes **408 government records across 15 series**, not 408 data centers. The units include city permits and plan reviews, distinct Arizona DEQ actions, undated county permit references, a proposed air permit, a dust-violation notice, and a denied rezoning. **Where to search.** The [PNNL IM3 Data Center Atlas](https://im3.pnnl.gov/datacenter-atlas) supplies the same research proxy as Texas. Its [November 5, 2026 source commit](https://github.com/IMMM-SFA/datacenter-atlas/blob/fe4d8420f9fdc7b16b82b8461c5e4c39b93d8f1d/static/im3_datacenter_centroids.geojson) contains 71 Arizona markers: 47 buildings, 10 campuses and 14 points. Maricopa has 69; Pima and Cochise each have one. A point-in-polygon join against the [2024 Census Arizona place polygons](https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/GENZ2024/shp/cb_2024_04_place_500k.zip) locates all 71 in incorporated places:

These counts guide discovery. They mix buildings, campuses and point-only names; for example, some labels name software or business-service firms rather than independently documented server facilities. A large campus may contribute several markers. They are not independently verified facility counts, planned projects, electricity demand, or operational megawatts. Generalized 2024 place boundaries are approximate and can predate annexations. A state/county permit's site city is not proof of municipal jurisdiction. 51 Arizona DEQ actions with no stated city remain at state level.

**What a record can tell you.** [Goodyear's PHX73 building shell](https://aca-prod.accela.com/GOODYEAR/Cap/CapDetail.aspx?Module=Building&TabName=Building&capID1=REC26&capID2=00000&capID3=002OF&agencyCode=GOODYEAR) identifies the server halls and calls the shell permit issued. It is not evidence that the completed building operates or the associated water, power or air decisions all passed. Phoenix CGD 21004899 is a grading/drainage civil permit for an explicitly named data center, not its main building authorization. Classification of post-issuance events matters: Mesa sometimes populates a finaled timestamp on a still-in-review record. Phoenix can report OPEN alongside a completion date or past expiration. Keep original current status, all separate lifecycle fields and the event date role; do not infer legal validity from a capitalized status.

**How matching works.** The collector requests broad sector strings, then requires an explicit data center/centre, computer center, data/server hall or server farm term in relevant fields. A Chandler/Goodyear portal shorthand DATACTR qualifies. The permit registry does not admit an applicant-name-only or generic warehouse match. Two location-qualified water aliases are allowed: Project Blue in Tucson (the [county identifies it as unincorporated](https://www.pima.gov/project-blue-faq)) and PHX73 in Goodyear with the municipal shell record as corroboration. A city string on a state filing remains a source label. No new construction is inferred from the presence of a facility name. Some records concern an accessory trailer, site lighting, a right-of-way installation, a renovation or an interior data room rather than a principal facility. The [excluded candidates](../data/history/arizona/exclusions.json) retain IDs and reasons.

Scottsdale used a different method. We screened the entire city's 288,158-row CSV against two county-documented facilities by parcel or address. The [city table](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=68ba7b38073f4cd4aee38d2b59afcaf4) supplies permit types, issue dates, owners and status, but no sector; a neighboring or former parcel occupant can be a false match. Only F002278 has a facility-specific name suitable for the strict owner/parcel/address cross-check. We excluded workers and owners associated only with a multi-use operations site, residential names and all address-only hits; other candidate rows remain outside the timeline. The archived subset carries a hash of the full 88 MB original and the number of rows screened, avoiding distribution of the entire city's personal/owner register.

**Archive, refresh and scope.** [Configuration](../data/history/arizona/config.json) pins the lab commit, Census vintage, source-field mappings and case document URL. The [collector](../scripts/collectors/arizona/history.py) uses existing Texas HTTP, GIS polygon and hashing helpers. Replay validates the compressed/downloaded bytes, reconciles county/municipal IDs against all candidate responses, checks the denial text in the signed Chandler minutes, and retains original portal statuses. The Maricopa county view generates temporary ESRI_OID values; using them as permanent IDs can return unrelated businesses. The collector independently reconciles the full matched rowset on physical OBJECTID, facility ID and P-number, then publishes durable facility/permit citations. ADEQ's aliases can produce several rows with one authorization number, event type and date; matched names and source IDs stay linked under one event. Place, permit and source totals are validated separately.

```sh
python3 scripts/collectors/arizona/history.py                         # offline replay; write data, rankings and validation totals
python3 scripts/collectors/arizona/history.py --refresh               # refresh all bulk sources and the pinned list of municipal record sheets
python3 scripts/collectors/arizona/history.py --refresh --reuse-accela # refresh bulk sources while keeping slow portal pages at their original snapshot
```

Use a project copy to retain prior snapshots before refreshing. The source descriptions for Scottsdale and Maricopa are from city/county-published ArcGIS feeds, accessible via the publisher's official Hub download or GIS. Seed discovery for Chandler/Goodyear used [SAVRN](https://savrn.com/data-center-permits-tracker/arizona) and two AZ project indexes ([PHX73](https://arizonaai.com/data-centers/microsoft-phx-73), [Goodyear campus](https://arizonaai.com/data-centers/microsoft-goodyear-campus)). No dates or statuses were taken from those directories. The public portal returned a rate limit during discovery; 34 already acquired record pages were inspected, and [29 deferred links](../data/history/arizona/accela_deferred.json) remain separate. The two county notice documents were readable via the web reader although direct automated downloads returned 403; the [pinned reviewed excerpt](../cache/history/az/reviewed-notices.json) identifies exactly what was seen and requires manual re-review on later refreshes. Pima's agency-issued press bulletin is independently archived in full. No automated attempt was made to get around these access restrictions.

Coverage is strongest in Mesa, Phoenix and the statewide stormwater program. Cochise/Benson has no verified local historical decision here. Smaller cities and counties still need municipal zoning, building, water-supply and individual permit searches; the [coverage ledger](../data/history/arizona/coverage.json) records the specific gaps. A zero return is a result of the named data source and selection criteria, not a negative legal or permitting determination.
