# Virginia water, stormwater and wetlands records

The Nov. 15, 2026 Eastern retrieval produced **447 source records** across seven public registers. These are permit actions, wetland records, determinations and application workflows; they are **not 447 permits or facilities**. The six complete GIS exports came from Virginia DEQ's ArcGIS publisher. The seventh register is the Commonwealth's [Virginia Permit Transparency (VPT) search](https://permits.virginia.gov/Permit/Search). The [machine-readable manifest](../data/history/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits/manifest.json) gives exact UTC retrieval times (Nov. 16 UTC), hashes, scans, query counts and stable dataset URLs. The source systems do not share a reporting cutoff. The maximum *displayed date for a matched row* is Oct. 9, 2026; that is not a claim that permitting stopped then. The GIS export and the VPT workflow can lag, include different time spans and disagree. A catalog's last-modified timestamp is not its records' effective date.

**What the records show.** [Project Loch's VPT application](https://permits.virginia.gov/Permit/Details/129165) reports an individual VWP application received Feb. 3, 2026 and still in progress in this snapshot. DEQ's GIS classification for the same number, 26-0171, is Application; its separate SSWD-000659 has a May 19 determination approval date. These are three useful records about the project, not three final approvals. The [U.S. Army Corps of Engineers public notice](https://www.nao.usace.army.mil/Media/Public-Notices/Article/4484789/nao-2026-00182-vmrc-project-loch-chesterfield-county-virginia-wauford-prm-site/) solicits comments on the federal proposal; the [Corps notice for Raspberry](https://www.nao.usace.army.mil/Media/Public-Notices/Article/4428952/nao-2025-01857-project-raspberry-botetourt-virginia/) does likewise. Neither is a signed final permit. The outward-facing Norfolk District tag index returned HTTP 403 to the collector, so no scraped federal notice or federal decision is counted. The [CXE01 Mullin workflow](https://permits.virginia.gov/Permit/Details/172261) says Complete; the field measures workflow completion and is not converted to a permit decision. A VMRC permit type may itself say “No VMRC Permit Nec.” even when its workflow status is In Progress. Preserve both fields.

**Stormwater timing and administration.** Output includes 116 PER_ISSUED, 97 PER_REISS, 110 MODIFIED, 25 TRANSFER and 17 PER_TERM records, plus 11 submitted or in-progress entries. These are counts of source actions and can overlap on a permit. DEQ's [field dictionary](https://gisdata.deq.virginia.gov/mapper_ext/DataFactSheets/Water_Permitting/StormwaterPermitsDEQFactSheet.pdf) identifies the letter-sent field as the issue date and the effective field as the *five-year program cycle* start. The issue field is blank in every retained row. The viewer instead labels available signed dates, application receipts or termination dates. The program authority (including local government versus DEQ) and site city label remain separate. A named local authority is a program administrator, not evidence of a local zoning approval. DEQ-administered sites retain the raw site city and an unresolved local jurisdiction; no legal boundary is inferred from a mailing city. Local erosion permits and local land-disturbance approvals often require a separate locality search.

**Selection and blind spots.** All 58,531 records of the six exports were tested against an explicit data center/centre name (spacing, hyphen, plural and “datacenter” variants). The scope includes facilities, operator/applicant names and, for construction, the nature of the work. It can include supporting infrastructure; an applicant-only hit is association with a named data-center business, not verified proof of the site's end use. Two opaque project names are eligible only in their sourced locality: [Loch in Chesterfield](https://www.deq.virginia.gov/news-info/shortcuts/controversial-permits/project-loch) and [Raspberry in Botetourt](https://www.deq.virginia.gov/news-info/shortcuts/topics-of-interest/google-s-project-raspberry). Eight adjacent actions were included by exact source permit number. In VPT the public text box searched “data” and the two aliases, all agencies/statuses, then the script screened the agency program and the facility or applicant. It ignores an agent-only hit. The manifest records pagination totals. See [configuration](../data/history/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits/sources.json) for program names. There was no match in the VPT groundwater withdrawal program under that screen; private wells for drinking/domestic use are a distinct VDH category. The [DEQ withdrawal program](https://www.deq.virginia.gov/water/water-withdrawal) and utility supply contracts are not a register of which data center consumes a water supplier’s volume.

Each output has a source/layer and locator. A GIS record number identifies the permit/case; its object ID points into the downloaded snapshot and can change on republish. CGP action identity uses the SWC action ID, not the shared permit number; VPT uses its published workflow ID. The same number can legitimately occur in both GIS and VPT and in several workflow types. Do not sum unique numbers across registers or infer a single site from matching strings. The data do not cover unnamed data centers, every local land-disturbance permit, agency files that are not in GIS, drinking-water customers, separate federal permits, all versions of superseded records, or all permits for an identified project. The state GIS is a reference index, not the signed instrument.

**Reproduce.** From the project directory:

```sh
python3 scripts/collectors/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits.py --verify        # reparse cached source bytes and compare every output and hash
python3 scripts/collectors/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits.py                # rebuild from snapshots, offline
python3 scripts/collectors/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits.py --refresh      # replace snapshots with live official exports and VPT responses, rebuild
python3 scripts/collectors/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits.py --refresh-vpt  # refresh public VPT search only and rebuild
```

The [collector](../scripts/collectors/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits.py) accepts `--config`, `--cache`, `--output` and `--manifest` for an isolated rerun. Configure a **new cache** for new snapshots if earlier time points must be retained. Default output is [the source-level JSON](../output/history/virginia/statewide/deq_water_permits.json); default raw archive is [cache/history/water](../cache/history/water/). The archive preserves complete official exports including their public administrative columns; the viewer omits contact details. A SHA-256 check is proof of local reproduction against a saved manifest, not proof that an official record remains current. On this run the DEQ EDMA map service returned a JSON service-not-found response to direct requests; the agency's catalogued ArcGIS Hub CSV download remained accessible. VPT uses the same public datatable POST as the published search form; direct links point to individual records.

**Porting.** Date parsing, field-based matching, permit-number history expansion, paginated search total checks, content hashing, and the output schema can be reused. The ArcGIS Hub endpoint pattern is reusable for jurisdictions publishing ArcGIS items; other providers need a new adapter. Six layer keys, CEDS column names, locality checks, issuer verification, SWC event/date semantics and VPT's form/program vocabulary are Virginia-specific. A neighboring state needs its own source ID/field map, status/action definitions, stable event key, jurisdiction/agency roles, and tests for a pending, issued and terminated record before aggregating it. Passing a different state’s URLs alone will not implement those semantics.
