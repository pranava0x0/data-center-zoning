# Ten-state queue and expansion evidence

Survey date: **2026-11-15** (Eastern). The first pass covered the ten requested states; Missouri is outside the sample. Virginia and Loudoun County are the pilot. This release's non-Virginia sample tests state water, stormwater, air and permitting programs against concrete municipal, township, county and parish examples. It is not an inventory of every applicable rule for every community. The canonical record file is [expansion.json](../data/expansion.json); consult the build’s validation column before relying on any row.

## Priority depends on the question being asked

The shipped worklist defaults to combined operating, under-construction and planned **facility counts**, described below. For a queue based on projected power, we separately rank 2030 medium-case **nominal IT capacity** using [EPRI’s original study](https://restservice.epri.com/publicdownload/000000003002034696/0/Product), [Table 2 and scenario definitions](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/nominal-capacity.html) and [the authors’ data dashboard](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/dashboard/). EPRI synthesizes industry sources and models realization of projects at different stages. The metric includes small and large facilities and cryptocurrency mining. The low/high cases are scenarios, not confidence intervals. Never add the 2024 operating estimate to the 2030 total: the latter already includes the base. Nominal IT capacity is distinct from peak grid load or annual energy use.

For a literal **operating-plus-project count**, the independent [US Data Center Map publisher table](https://dcmap.us/states/) reports operating, under construction and planned entries as of October 2026. These are facilities/projects according to that directory, not a government census or independently verified counts. Its [methodology](https://dcmap.us/methodology/) explains how published power and stages are assigned; data may count buildings and campuses differently across sources. Its [national overview](https://dcmap.us/) reports power figures for only 1,810 entries/figures against 4,771 current facilities and projects, with some figures covering a future phase of an operating site. Reported power mixes definitions and stages; do not compare it numerically with EPRI IT capacity as if both measured the same thing. [Pew's separately sourced February comparison](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/top-states-data-centers/) also put Virginia and Texas first by combined counts but supplies only the top 15 states and uses a broader planned bucket including land-banked projects; it was not spliced into this series.

By inventory count alone: VA, TX, OH, AZ, IN, TN, NV, MS, LA/NM tied. By the same directory's **published/announced power**, the order is TX, VA, OH, NM, AZ, IN, NV, LA, TN, MS. New Mexico thus merits an early high-uncertainty spot check despite its last-place EPRI ranking. Indiana and Ohio swap if you use EPRI's low case. Raw queue, all three sortable ranks, all six EPRI columns and the directory counts are in [state_priority.csv](../data/state_priority.csv). Rank denominators are the ten requested states only.

[rank_states.py](../scripts/pipeline/rank_states.py) extracts the three EPRI scenarios directly from the published dashboard's version-pinned data bundle. It reads numbers without executing downloaded JS, converts GW to MW using decimal arithmetic, checks each state and scenario and verifies that directory stages sum to total. Run `python3 scripts/pipeline/rank_states.py --check` for a deterministic offline audit; without flags it regenerates the CSV. `--refresh --check` checks the same edition online and stops on changed bytes. The pinned image of the original [Table 2](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/assets/Table_2_web-B6Lz8vbF.png) in the priority cache independently permits visual comparison. The ten directory rows are a dated transcription of the publisher's rendered table, not a facility-level scrape or a claim that future editions have the same counts.

## What the validator can and cannot establish

A text anchor establishes provenance for a record, not the absence of an amendment or automatic eligibility of a parcel. The Lake and Santa Fe adopted PDFs are scans. Their page-specific readings are in [visual_reviews.json](../data/visual_reviews.json), with the archived bytes’ hashes and reviewer attribution; none is an OCR or independent legal sign-off. For blocked sources, never bypass the publisher's robots policy; keep the row held, or locate a separately published official instrument. A failed host is not “no ordinance.”

Further manual work should prioritize: parcel/overlay review in New Albany; full permit matrix from College Station's adopted attachment; duration and amendments for the Texas state screening and local moratoria; township/city limits and independent municipalities before assigning county rules; and delegated tribal, air-district, sewer and special-district boundaries before asserting permit applicability. [Fort Worth's process page](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/city-manager/datacenters) includes future scheduled actions; [Jackson's Nov. 3 agenda packet](https://jacksonms.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Regular-City-Council-Agenda-Packet-11.3.2026.pdf) includes proposed repeal text. Neither by itself proves adoption. No parcel determinations or full-code negative findings are claimed here.
