# Cross-state validation against COMPASS

This atlas adopts NLR’s **organization of evidence**, not other technologies’ zoning rules. In the [transmission release](https://data.openei.org/submissions/8787), NLR publishes a jurisdiction search list separately from extracted ordinance features, preserves a source and section for the extraction, and omits state rules from the geometry package built for reVX. The [NLR solar tools page](https://www.nlr.gov/gis/solar-supply-curves) describes an earlier locality-by-locality review with citations. NLR’s [formal validation](https://natlabrockies.github.io/COMPASS/val/validation.html) evaluates web discovery separately from extraction on already supplied documents. The [research inventory](compass.md) traces five published technology datasets. The [crosscheck report](../output/compass_crosscheck.json) reconciles all five audited workbooks (34,466 feature rows) with the pinned catalog and compares Virginia, Ohio, Texas, Arizona and Indiana using the following distinct units.

**Meaningful legal differences within shared families.** [Virginia’s groundwater law](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title62.1/chapter25/section62.1-259/) excludes withdrawals below 300,000 gallons per month and those outside a declared management area, among other exceptions. [Ohio’s statute](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1521.23) instead concerns new or increased *consumptive use* averaging **more than** two million gallons/day over a 30-day period, subject to its own exceptions. These are separate keys, units, operators and issuer territories. [Loudoun’s grading guidance](https://www.loudoun.gov/6244/Land-Disturbance) sets a county grading screen in square feet with sensitive-area rules, including distinct floodplain treatment. [Texas’s TXR150000 guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/stormwater/construction/TXR15_AIR.html) instead uses acres disturbed across a common plan and distinguishes small and large construction discharges. No Virginia grading threshold can fill a missing Texas stormwater field.

[New Albany’s official FAQ](https://datacenters.newalbanyohio.org/resident-questions/) distinguishes GE/TMD base zoning from site-specific rezonings that excluded data centers. [San Marcos’s council recap](https://www.sanmarcostx.gov/4772/June-16-2026-City-Council-Recap) reports a prohibition in its own city zoning districts. Keep those city-level outcomes and document types. A TCEQ agency pause and a township zoning moratorium receive different families. Neither transfers to another state, and neither follows from a county having no extracted row.

**Arizona and Indiana.** Arizona’s [A.R.S. §45-512](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/45/00512.htm) concerns withdrawal from nonexempt wells in active management areas with specified exceptions; industrial use is one permit category. [Chandler’s zoning bulletin](https://www.chandleraz.gov/news-center/chandlers-data-center-ordinance-now-effect) distinguishes the PAD rule for primary uses from ancillary facilities. Neither rule travels outside its legal territory. [Indiana DNR](https://www.in.gov/dnr/water/water-availability-use-rights/significant-water-withdrawal-facility-registration/) defines the threshold for **registration** using aggregate *capability* exceeding 100,000 gallons/day, not actual consumption or a withdrawal permit. [Boone County ordinance 2026-08](https://boonecounty.in.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Ordinance-2026-08.Moratorium-on-data-centers.pdf), pp. 1–2, is a temporary pause in the county’s unincorporated jurisdiction, with an internal-IT exclusion and a stated June 15, 2027 end date. Both source roles and qualifiers are checked in the fixtures. The [permit-status contract](permit-status.md) documents the separate map metric for distinct permits.

**Transfer tests prompted by actual records.** Fort Worth surfaced two previously unmapped activities. Its [commercial permitting guide](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/development-services/permits/commercial) places accessory structures in the building process; its [urban forestry program](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/development-services/urbanforestry) administers tree preservation and removal. The taxonomy distinguishes those subjects; neither observation establishes a Virginia permit requirement. [TDLR’s Texas project registration](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/abfaq.htm) documents an architectural-barriers filing; it does not confirm ADA compliance or issue a building permit. [Virginia's existing-building accessibility provisions](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section432.5/) distinguish change of occupancy, additions and alterations and contain specific exceptions. For example, [TDLR TABS2025010215](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Projects/TABS2025010215) records registration on January 23, 2025 and a closed project status. Its work is an operations facility serving neighboring data centers, not itself a reported data hall. The Virginia substantive requirements use the building/life-safety rule family, not the Texas registration procedure. Virginia demolition questions start with [Virginia's own permit provisions](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section80/), not a Texas filing or a generic trade code.

Ohio exposed another ambiguous label: its [wastewater permit-to-install program](https://epa.ohio.gov/divisions-and-offices/surface-water/permitting/wastewater-permit-to-install-pti-program) authorizes infrastructure installation, whereas its [construction stormwater register](https://epa.ohio.gov/divisions-and-offices/surface-water/permitting/stormwater-program) tracks NPDES coverage. The pilot contains one sewer PTI and 52 construction NPDES records. Also distinguish a proposal from an effective instrument: [Ohio EPA’s general permit listing](https://epa.ohio.gov/divisions-and-offices/surface-water/permitting/npdes-general-permits) says it did not finalize the proposed data-center NPDES general permit. This does not waive any applicable individual-discharge or construction stormwater permitting. A withdrawn New Albany zoning case may even have a GIS field called AdoptionDate; the atlas reports the field as a **disposition date** and retains the withdrawal. Fort Worth rows likewise retain status or filing dates. An Ohio or Texas event with a status date cannot be compared as an issue date with an Austin issued-permit entry.

**Audit findings.** After adding Virginia accessibility and demolition there are 38 published Virginia provisions, four Ohio and eight Texas; the live report breaks out held candidates separately. The frame has 361, 2,326 and 1,479 respective geography rows; it is not a count of distinct independent permitting authorities. Ohio and Texas have substantially fewer verified provisions than Virginia. The initial three-state combined history had 3,593 source rows (2,802 Virginia, 141 Ohio, 650 Texas). Rerun strict validation for the current Arizona and Indiana additions; the linked JSON is authoritative for counts. The live JSON provides separate counts by regulatory family, raw register and broad stage; adding project histories cannot increase ordinance coverage. Ten purpose-selected [source fixtures](../data/compass_validation_cases.json), two per state, check district scopes, source roles, numeric definitions and qualifiers against primary materials. This is an agent-authored regression sample, not blinded independent legal review or a statistical accuracy estimate. Check date exclusions, amendments, delegated authority and the controlling text independently before applying an individual rule.

**Classification contract.** [The vocabulary](../scripts/pipeline/compass_taxonomy.py) exposes `classify_rule(topic)`, `classify_history(kind, series=…, program=…, dataset=…)` and `classify_stage(kind,status)`. Rule families distinguish land use, authority, construction, building/life safety, air/power, site standards, and water/wastewater. For history, building trades, financial guarantees, administrative correspondence, wetland activity, stormwater, and water withdrawals are separate. Source labels and dates survive intact. “Complete” can denote an administrative workflow; a registered accessibility project is not a building permit; a State Surface Waters Determination documents delineation; a source's issue date is not the latest status date. Unknown historical terms go to **Unclassified — review**. First inspect the issuer’s dictionary and a primary case, then add a narrowly scoped alias with a contrary example. Never classify from an operator’s name or the proposed project’s scale.

**Run from the project folder:**

```sh
python3 scripts/pipeline/validate_compass.py --strict-taxonomy
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_compass_validation.py'
```

The validator reads the assembled rules, held/published CSVs, coverage inventory, numerical candidates, pinned NLR audit, and combined history CSV. It fails on cross-state joins, offered but unsupported geographies, candidate/digest mismatches, missing or overlapping publication partitions, missing required citation fields, regression fixture changes, and (with the flag) unmapped historical labels. It preserves the two separately dated Botetourt events whose official source states no case number; a digest and at least a month are required for unnumbered events. To validate new source arrays before the combined build, append `--extra-history OH=output/history/ohio/history.json --extra-history TX=output/history/texas/history.json`; do not pass files which do not yet exist. The JSON is written each run even if a check fails. An explicitly supplied complete state array replaces that state’s prior assembled rows for this check. This avoids counting superseded IDs after an adapter refines its event key.

**Recommended next sample.** For a performance measure comparable in structure to NLR’s, date and freeze a stratified jurisdiction sample including blocked portals, small townships, independent cities and areas served by separate water authorities. Have two independent reviewers label controlling documents, legal status, district/threshold/exception fields and relevant event types with adjudication. Report discovery precision/recall and field-level agreement separately by state, source type and version, with denominators. No script result here estimates discovery recall or legal completeness.
