# FAST-41: actual agency milestones, not permit counts

The [federal full dataset](https://data.permits.performance.gov/Permitting-Project/Permitting-Dashboard-Full-Dataset/mcm3-xbid/about_data) contained 24,313 rows for 1,771 projects at extraction. All projects were grouped and counted, and the group totals reconciled to the complete dataset. We screened titles, both sector fields, and action descriptions for data-center and computing vocabulary. Two verified projects matched: Virginia's [QTS Richmond DC5](https://www.permits.performance.gov/permitting-project/fast-41-covered-projects/qts-richmond-technology-park-data-center-5-ric5) and Ohio's [PORTS Technology Campus](https://www.permits.performance.gov/permitting-project/fast-41-covered-projects/ports-technology-campus). The [Council portfolio](https://www.permitting.gov/projects/current-fast-41-portfolio) independently lists both; PORTS appears there as Portsmouth Data Center. The register is limited to tracked federal projects. It is not a census of federal permits or of data centers.

Across eight actions, the two projects had 38 timetable entries. Eighteen have both an actual completion date through November 15, 2026 and a completed flag. Twenty are not published as completed history, including the final Corps decision for **both** projects: target date, baseline and actual completion are distinct columns. One separate signed Council scheduling decision gives 19 published events. The snapshot captured the current dataset after the cutoff and filters actual event dates; it is not a time-travel reconstruction of the database as it looked on November 15. The dated scheduling decision is available [directly from the Council](https://www.permits.performance.gov/sites/permits.dot.gov/files/2026-09/PORTS%20Technology%20Campus%20Executive%20Director%20Determination%209-28-2026.pdf); the new January 29, 2027 date is **not** an approval. The archived extract identifies pages 1–2 where the public original could be checked but its host would not provide an automated download.

Virginia's [Norfolk District public notice](https://www.nao.usace.army.mil/Media/Public-Notices/Article/4510991/nao-2021-02695-qts-ric-5-henrico-virginia/) identifies DA case NAO-2021-02695. The Dashboard appends -RRS. The [FWS outcome](https://www.permits.performance.gov/proj/qts-richmond-technology-park-data-center-5-ric5/endangered-species-act-consultation-doi-fws) is a concurrence letter on July 2; Ohio's [FWS outcome](https://www.permits.performance.gov/proj/ports-technology-campus/endangered-species-act-consultation-doi-fws) is a biological opinion on September 11. Both dates correspond specifically to the completed consultation milestone. We did not obtain the signed FWS instruments; the regulator-reported outcomes do not establish construction permission. A completed Section 106 consultation likewise establishes no new permit. Ohio's Corps file is LRH-2026-00354-SCR. The Corps ORM evidence does not establish issued permits for either file. The data retains file numbers for cross-checks, with **no new permit representatives** here.

The stable natural key is the federal milestone ID. Each row links to the government's single-record Socrata response, its action and project pages, the responsible agency, and the checksum of the source rows. A retrospective project outcome appears only on the matching **final** milestone: earlier records keep status Completed. The OH determination is a separate dated document without an invented permit number. `details.program` carries the actual federal action type, so the viewer can distinguish wetlands, endangered species, historic preservation, environmental review and timetable administration. The dataset's “canonical” flag is a group representative (one for the project and one for each action). It does not invalidate the other distinct milestones.

Run `python3 scripts/collectors/federal/fast41_dashboard.py --refresh` to fetch the public Socrata tables and Council portfolio, verify the full count, and serialize new snapshots. A previously unseen candidate project stops the refresh for review. `python3 scripts/collectors/federal/fast41_dashboard.py` is offline and validates the checksums and census totals. The machine-readable audit and holds are in `data/federal_dashboard_*.json`; the two series are `output/history/federal/fast41_dashboard/{ohio,virginia}.json`. Test with `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_federal_dashboard.py`.
