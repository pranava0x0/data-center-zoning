# What the National Laboratory of the Rockies actually publishes

Research snapshot: **November 15, 2026**. The [COMPASS viewer](https://compass.nlr.gov/) exposes five technology choices and distinguishes state data from local jurisdiction data. COMPASS is the collection and extraction software; the viewer displays results, and [Siting Lab/reV](https://www.nlr.gov/gis/rev) uses ordinance data in a larger geospatial modeling workflow. The lab's software is BSD-3-Clause; the audited OEDI releases specify CC BY 4.0. This independent data-center project does not reproduce NLR's map or treat NLR's energy data as data-center law.

[Machine-readable source catalog](../data/compass_sources.json): full distribution links, workbook SHA-256 digests, actual headers, counts by feature, search-manifest hashes, collection versions, primary papers and documentation. The five workbooks together are about **9.64 MB** (decimal), covering 34,466 feature rows. The compact size does not include raw ordinance documents, GIS geometry, or historic versions. For comparison the transmission release advertises an approximately 86 MB search GeoPackage and a compressed search CSV of only 241,249 bytes. Do not copy geometry into every rule; join it on demand using jurisdiction IDs and a boundary vintage.

Each release has a workbook with the consolidated table, geography-specific views, column descriptions and an ordinance-type dictionary. Do not concatenate all worksheets; that would double-count records. Do not use Excel's maximum row to count findings: some sheets have thousands of formatted but empty rows. The GIS product attaches local shapes for reVX; **it omits state-level rules**. Use the workbook to reconcile state coverage. The [county FIPS raster](https://data.openei.org/submissions/8397) is a modeling input, not a law or a substitute for municipal boundaries.

The public inventory is not identical to either the README or the list of supported software modules. [The source snapshot audited](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/COMPASS/tree/91fda9734cc345b9e441424b59903350afc046b8) was committed Nov. 11; its README lists four releases, omits transmission, and the geothermal submission IDs in it returned generic New Submission pages. The live OEDI catalog and the underlying [GDR electricity](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1877) and [heat-pump](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1876) releases supplied actual files. Good validation checks for the expected document title, format and content as well as HTTP status.

## Papers, notes, and technologies beyond the five layers

* **Manual baseline.** The [2019 wind collection](https://data.openei.org/submissions/1932) and [2022 wind](https://data.openei.org/submissions/5733) combined cited materials from WindExchange, state-legislature research and legal scholarship; [2022 solar](https://data.openei.org/submissions/5734) followed literature review with review by locality. The [2022 lab release](https://www.nlr.gov/news/detail/program/2022/nrel-releases-comprehensive-databases-of-local-ordinances-for-siting-wind-solar-energy-projects) paired spreadsheets with interactive maps. These were predecessors, not additional 2025 automated observations.
* **Why normalize numbers.** [Lopez et al., Nature Energy (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-023-01319-3), the [associated briefing](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy23osti/85461.pdf), and the [solar land-use final report](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/91812.pdf) connect setbacks to GIS-based estimates of land availability. A modeled or extrapolated setback scenario is not an enacted ordinance. Likewise, a data-center screening layer must not label an entire county “permitted” because a single district allows the use.
* **How the extraction was evaluated.** [Buster et al., Energy and AI (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.egyai.2024.100431) used focused questions in a decision tree and reported roughly 85–90% accuracy on the wind extraction task. A repeatability test over five counties found average variability of 4.8% across ten repetitions even at temperature zero. A recorded model setting is insufficient for deterministic replication: retain original sources, candidates, decisions and reviewer-approved mappings.
* **Energy-storage work is not a sixth public COMPASS layer.** An [earlier Solar Prize report](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/95408.pdf) scopes permitting and interconnection; the [2026 final report, pp. 4–5](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/3388180) describes manual additions of solar district definitions/map links. Because COMPASS had not then been developed for storage, researchers manually researched Texas battery siting and found three municipal requirements, supplying the partner a workbook. Partner work is not evidence of a public national battery release.
* **Source modules vs. releases.** The [documentation](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/COMPASS/blob/91fda9734cc345b9e441424b59903350afc046b8/docs/source/misc/about.rst) also describes small/distributed wind (e.g., rated capacity and fees), and Texas water-rights multi-document extraction (e.g., withdrawal periods, metering, export and well spacing). The pinned [extraction directory](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/COMPASS/tree/91fda9734cc345b9e441424b59903350afc046b8/compass/extraction) also includes schemas for gas-fired electric generation, gas pipelines, oil/gas wells and federal BLM geothermal Resource Management Plans. These are separate scopes, with no separate national workbook identified in the five viewer layers. The documentation lists data centers in development; no released data-center layer was found here. An on-site emergency generator is not automatically a utility power plant.
* **Data-center-specific starting point.** Frank Oteri's NLR [Common Elements of Data Center Ordinances, slides 2–4](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/gen/fy27/102426.pdf) discusses zoning pathways and sensitive receptors, sound measurement, public infrastructure, cooling, backup generation, and decommissioning. It distinguishes water supply from wastewater discharge and ongoing noise from generator testing. This is a research taxonomy and discovery checklist; cite the adopting authority when recording a requirement.

## Collection and verification

The [technical notes](https://natlabrockies.github.io/COMPASS/misc/about.html) describe four ways in: a known document, a known URL, targeted web search or a crawl of the official jurisdiction site. Given a jurisdiction roster, COMPASS filters candidates by issuing jurisdiction, whether the text is legally operative, and technology relevance. It screens chunks cheaply, retains relevant passages, checks cleaned text against the original and drops persistent mismatches. Feature-specific decision trees then ask narrow questions about values, units and conditions. A [one-shot schema example](https://github.com/NatLabRockies/COMPASS/tree/91fda9734cc345b9e441424b59903350afc046b8/examples/one_shot_schema_extraction) provides an alternative to a full plug-in. These are design options, not evidence that a correct citation proves a correct interpretation.

The [lab's stage-specific validation report and confusion matrices](https://natlabrockies.github.io/COMPASS/val/validation.html) quantify why a “verified” label needs a definition:

| Task evaluated | Evaluation set | Precision | Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Wind document discovery (Sep 2025) | 100 documents | 82.00% | 74.55% |
| Solar feature extraction on supplied documents (Aug 2025) | 78 documents, 1,794 feature decisions | 84.01% | 84.01% |
| Geothermal electricity document discovery (May 2026) | 100 documents | 85.71% | 80.00% |
| Heat-pump document discovery (Jun 2026) | 50 documents | 100.00% | 91.67% |
| Transmission document discovery (Jun 2026) | 50 documents | 70.00% | 60.87% |

Do **not** transfer these figures to data-center permits, or confuse recall of discovery with accuracy of field extraction. Measure (1) document discovery against independently identified controlling documents, (2) field agreement against manually labeled legal provisions, and (3) version/effective-date and supersession errors separately. Include wrong jurisdictions, agenda drafts, text copied from an applicant, an expired moratorium, exceptions and pre-existing approvals as negative tests. Deliberately sample silent and inaccessible jurisdictions, not just successful harvests. Report numerator, denominator, adjudication procedure, date, and source type; do not use a passing software test as a precision estimate.

## Crosswalk for a data-center equivalent

This is a design crosswalk, including fields needed for extensions; it is not a claim that every field is populated in the pilot.

| COMPASS pattern or field | Retain or adapt | Why it changes a screening decision |
|---|---|---|
| State, County, Subdivision, Jurisdiction Type, subdivision FIPS | Typed issuing authority, legal territory, jurisdiction ID and boundary vintage; keep state/county/place/county-subdivision/special district distinct. | A city ordinance is not countywide. A water provider can overlap several governments. An independent city is a county equivalent, not a child of the neighboring county. |
| Feature: primary/special/accessory/prohibited use districts | Preserve the quoted use definition, district code, district-specific pathway, size trigger and exception; use separate rows for different legal outcomes. | A principal use can be by right in one district but discretionary in another. Industrial classification alone proves neither one. |
| Value, Units, Summary, setback adder and min/max | Preserve operator, unit, measurement point, time period, equipment and alternatives; allow qualitative rules. | Gross floor area is not IT load. A construction disturbance threshold is not a campus area cap; average dBA over time is not instantaneous noise. |
| Section, Ordinance Year, Source URL | Keep official document URL plus locator/excerpt, content hash, observed date, adoption/effective/expiry dates when supported, and legal/document status. | Proposed amendments, guidance and project-specific conditions can all appear on the same official site. A year cannot resolve a grandfathering clause. |
| Searched-jurisdiction files separate from findings | Separate jurisdiction inventory, attempted fetch, candidate document, coverage scope, and accepted requirement. Explicitly distinguish missing/unreadable from searched/no evidence. | No rule found is not a finding of by-right use. A general permit can apply even if no document contains “data center.” |
| Local geometry and separate state records | Join provision to its issuing territory and show overlapping rules; keep the district map/source separately if available. | A map hit or a county centroid locates the issuing jurisdiction, not a permitted parcel. |
| Tech-specific decision tree and special studies | Branch first on zoning entitlement, then construction/land disturbance, air/generation, water withdrawal, discharge, electric service, life safety and operating conditions; record issuing agency and statutory trigger. | One project can need multiple approvals from different entities. Utility tariff, tax incentive, public guidance, signed permit and generally applicable ordinance must remain distinguishable. |

**Repeat the upstream audit.** Retrieve the catalog URL in the JSON, resolve its HTML JSON-LD `distribution[].contentUrl`, download the workbook and compare its SHA-256 to `workbook_audit.sha256`. Open the All Ordinances sheet, locate the row containing both State and Feature (there may be a notice above it), and count only populated rows satisfying both conditions. The other geography tabs are views; the last two describe columns and features. Verify the searched-jurisdiction CSV separately, decompressing the transmission file before counting data rows. Recheck the catalog and viewer at each release; retain old digests and capture dates so real changes can be distinguished from moved URLs.

The [source crosswalk](compass-code-sources.md) also follows the original workbook hyperlinks back to local codebooks. A wind or solar feature is never translated into a data-center rule. The local rule must be found independently in the government document.
